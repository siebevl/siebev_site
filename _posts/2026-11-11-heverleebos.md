This is a big one. [As noted in a previous post](https://www.siebev.com/2026/10/21/morning.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}, some Northern nutcrackers have been pouring into Belgium. The hype was so big it even made the [national news](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/27/zeldzame-vogel-spotten-landen-dunsnavelnotenkraker-hobbyfotograa/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. Specifically, it is the *macrorhynchos*/slender-billed/Siberian subspecies (multiple denominations, though slender-billed seems a funny choice given its scientific name). Normally found between the Ural mountains and north-east China, the slender-billed nutcracker can erupt out of its range when pine cone shortages occur, causing it to search for food as far as western Europe. There were some 300 nutcrackers recorded in 1968 in Great Britain, but the original source for this in the Wikipedia article on the northern nutcracker is no longer available. After dredging Google's muddied search results (there was also a movie release of "The Nutcracker" in the same year), [this archive article](https://britishbirds.co.uk/journal/article/invasion-nutcrackers-autumn-1968) puts the 1968 GB count of Northern nutcrackers at 315, with no specification of the subspecies. [This paper](https://www.dutchavifauna.nl/static/images/records/43593.pdf){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} (what am I, a scientist still?) recorded 4000 observations in The Netherlands between June 1968–June 1969 but gives no estimate on the total amount of birds, only noting that 100 of them were ringed. I did not find a source citing the number of individuals. In any case, the number of observations today has not paralleled that of 1968, but with almost 1200 observations this year to date we are well underway to have similar numbers to 1968. It's also worth noting that almost all (99.2%) of *macrorhynchos* observations on waarnemingen in The Netherlands since 1971 [occurred this year](https://waarneming.nl/species/87301/statistics/?year=2026&month=&sex=&life_stage=&activity=&method=&user=&location=)! So it's special, very special.

Northern nutcrackers occur in Europe every year, and the (less rare yet still rare) thick-billed (*nucifraga caryocatactes caryocatactes*) subspecies, its normal range being Europe, is [not seen more often this year than other years](https://waarnemingen.be/species/1604/statistics/?year=2023&month=&sex=&life_stage=&activity=&method=&location=&user=){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. The fact that the invasion is specifically by the slender-billed subspecies (*macrorhynchos*), with birds travelling all the way from Siberia, is worth some more emphasis than VRT gives it.

So naturally, I jump on a train taking me to Leuven, where I rent an electric bluebike on which I have horrible posture because the frames are too small for a normal human being of 1m83. Other than that and the resulting back pains, great service (no sponsor). I stop at 't Smullerke (no sponsor) for a broodje gerookte zalmsalade and make my way to Heverleebos (no sponsor), facing the 10,000 students on the Tiensebaan, none of whom could name a single traffic rule.

It's still a quiet moment in the year for birding and even though the sun is out, most birds are hard to spot because not all the leaves have fallen yet. First contact is with a majestic blue tit holding on to its nut for dear life.

Next I snag a slightly better picture [than previously](https://www.siebev.com/2026/10/31/cologne.html) of a crested tit and a [not-so-great-as-before](https://www.siebev.com/2026/10/11/hi.html) picture of a nuthatch. You win some, you lose some.

I walk around for a few hours before I decide to just go to the last known location of the nutcracker reported on waarnemingen.be. The first friendly birder I find there doesn't even need bribing with my nicnacskes to tell me that the bird was just there, but I give him some anyway. We stand around for a couple of minutes -- him, a man that has seen The Bird, and me, who's rarest bird found to date is a zitting cisticola -- talking about how he tried to spot the nutcracker three days in a row last week with no luck. He then leaves for the office (a birder on a Wednesday noon with a job hanging around Heverleebos? Must be nice). I decide to wait it out at the last known location, all the while keeping an eye on a group of about 6 people in the distance. As another man-with-camera walks by towards the group, I tell him to give me a sign should they see something. Even before he arrives, the group that til just now looked inert, jumps into a frenzy, pointing all sorts of lenses at a tree.

The Moment and I both arrive, commemorated by the following pictures.

It stays for a couple of minutes in the same tree on a branch about 3 meters from the ground, then moves to a couple of low branches in the forest, before sitting on a branch and on the ground a couple of meters from where I'm standing along with some ten other people. Lucky my camera has stabilisation, cause I'm shaking from the encounter. As noted by VRT, the bird is not shy at all: it is not used to seeing people, so it also does not perceive us as a threat (although who *would* be afraid of a guy handing out nicnacskes to strangers?).

Anything else I still experience that day has become irrelevant, even the frame size of the bluebike on the way home.
