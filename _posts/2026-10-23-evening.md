Since the August east Canada trip of 2026, there have been lurking in my mind a couple of unidentified birds. For some, I have an idea but no ID. In such cases, the bird enthusiasts over at r/whatsthisbird are always keen to help. I asked for IDs of the following east Canadian birds ranging from Toronto to Tadoussac.

Strong consensus on r/whatsthisbird that my hunch of a female [brown-headed cowbird](https://ebird.org/species/bnhcow){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} for UB1 (unknown bird #1) was correct: the streaking on the body does not contrast enough for a female red-winged blackbird. UB2 is a sparrow, but it could be [Lincoln's sparrow](https://ebird.org/species/linspa){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} or a swamp or song sparrow. Strong narrow streaking on the belly points to Lincoln's sparrow, but there's no reports of those in early August. A swamp sparrow is more likely. UB2 will remain unsolved without hearing the bird sing.

Likewise, UB3 remains a mystery. Flycatchers are a nightmare to distinguish if you don't hear them sing, especially if you only have a picture of their belly side. Even Peter, former Algonquin park guide that we met on the trail who was also looking at this very same bird, couldn't tell which bird it was. Maybe a future post should detail our lovely encounter with Peter.

UB4 has two plausible options: a breeding female [Blackpoll warbler](https://ebird.org/species/bkpwar){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} or immature female [pine warbler](https://ebird.org/species/pinwar) could work, but the overall lack of colour in the picture leads me to have reservations about definitively saying this is X or Y.

Thrushes give me ID headaches, so for UB5 we are lucky that reddit thinks this is a Swainson's thrush (*dwerglijster*). There's a faint pale eye ring, the spotting on the body seems to match, and I remember that my overall impression of this bird was that it was smaller than a blackbird. With some confidence we can therefore ID UB5 as [Swainson's thrush](https://ebird.org/species/swathr){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

UB6 can be a 'generic' [black-headed gull](https://ebird.org/species/bkhgul){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} (*kokmeeuw*), a [Franklin's gull](https://ebird.org/species/fragul){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} or [Bonaparte's gull](https://ebird.org/species/bongul){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} (*kleine kokmeeuw*). The picture does not allow to see whether there is a thick white eye ring or what the colour of the legs is (this picture was taken with my phone as we were on the whale watching boat at the time and I didn't dare take my camera out for risk of whale splashing), so we turn to the ranges of these gulls on allaboutbirds.org. On August 12 there's only one of those three [which occurs very regularly in Tadoussac](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/browse/filter/loc/ChIJy06QX8DVv0wRWZXLMjSDQxM/date/8-12/behavior/all/size/all/colors/all/sort/family/view/list-view){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. Additionally, I feel like my camera should be able to record the much wider white eye ring on Franklin's. UB6 is therefore a Bonaparte's gull, as also suggested by reddit.

[Least flycatcher](https://ebird.org/species/leafly){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} seems to work best for UB7, given the relatively bold pale eye ring (other flycatchers such as Adler's have thinner eye rings), the two pale wing bars and the light-coloured underside (others are more olive/buff). So least flycatcher for UB7 it is!

All in all, pulling 4 new bird species out of these pictures without accompanying sound bits is more than I had dared to hope for, bringing the species total to 151.
