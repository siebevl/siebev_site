In South Africa in 2017, I took many pictures of elephants, rhinoceros, giraffes and the likes. What's even more exciting, I took some pictures of birds. And I forgot, until now.

Let's start off with the coolest ones. I was happy to find this almost fake looking African fish eagle and this colours-are-unreal lilac-breasted roller:

{% include image-row.html images="/media/south-africa2017/afrikaanse-zeearend.JPG, /media/south-africa2017/vorkstaartscharrelaar.JPG" titles="African fish eagle in Limpopo on 10 July 2017. | Lilac-breasted roller on 11 July 2017." %}

Award for the most badass looking bird of the trip has to go to the tawny eagle (*aquila rapax rapax*). Luckily this cute forest canary was almost 2000 km away, as it would definitely be on the menu of the former.

{% include image-row.html images="/media/south-africa2017/savannearend.JPG, /media/south-africa2017/boskanarie.JPG" titles="Tawny eagle in Kruger National Park on 12 July 2017. | Forest canary a.k.a. tennis borb on Table Mountain, Cape Town on 18 July 2017." %}

On the southmost point of Africa, we found the only penguin species native to Africa (and the Old World as a whole): the aptly-named African penguin.

{% include image-row.html images="/media/south-africa2017/pinguin.JPG, /media/south-africa2017/pinguin2.JPG" titles="African penguins near Cape Point on 17 July 2017. | More African penguin!" %}

The startling blue on this greater blue-eared starling is insane, and the yellow-billed hornbill looks like something out of a fantasy.

{% include image-row.html images="/media/south-africa2017/glansspreeuw.JPG, /media/south-africa2017/geelsnaveltok.JPG" titles="Greater Blue-eared Starling in Kruger National (car)Park on 12 July 2017. | I doubt this Southern yellow-billed hornbill has got a driver's license." %}

In total, I manage to find 17 bird species to add to the life list, putting the total at 183 (I've found some extra species in the meantime since the nutcracker, but that's an update for another day).
