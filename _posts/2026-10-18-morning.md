The early bird has to drive to Antwerp on a Sunday morning, but the even earlier birder manages to get some birding done even before that. No groundbreaking observations but a very photogenic wren and a kestrel being harassed by two crows. The shot of the kestrel illustrates that other inconvenient truth a friendly birder holding a 600 mm lens told me recently: you can never have enough millimeters.

![Eurasian wren posing on a Sunday morning in Bourgoyen.](/media/winterkoning.jpeg)
*Eurasian wren posing on a Sunday morning in Bourgoyen on 18 October 2026.*

Monday mornings are extremely quiet once you get out in the fields compared to Sundays. Without human distractions, I manage to spot a female hen harrier flying north over the Oude Kale valley. Unlike what the Dutch name would have you believe (blauwe kiekendief), the female harrier is brown and not blue, with a distinctive white rump.

![Female hen harrier flying over Merendree on 20 October 2026.](/media/blauwe-kiekendief.jpeg)
*Female hen harrier flying over Merendree on 20 October 2026.*

Along the track I hear (multiple?) lesser spotted woodpeckers calling. I can't find them, so the picture provided with the audio bite is by Yves Hoebeke from waarnemingen.be. Unlike the far more common greater spotted woodpecker, the lesser does not have a red lower belly.
