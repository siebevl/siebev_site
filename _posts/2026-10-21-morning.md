Common crane (*grus grus*) migration through Europe is underway from Scandinavia and northern Germany to the south. There's scattered reports all over the eastern parts of Belgium, exploding in the last couple of days. Chances are slim that a lot of the action happens over Ghent, which would require eastern winds blowing birds west. With dominant west and south-west wind this week, it's highly unlikely that any cranes will be spotted over Bourgoyen. But I made fresh coffee and I have a warm jacket, so out we go.

No cranes yet. Luckily, there's always the cute long-tailed tit to fall back on. A lot of barnacle geese flying over and resting at Afgraving. If the map on Wikipedia is to be trusted, these are mostly the Siberian population of barnacle geese, which winters in The Netherlands, Germany and Belgium. Fun wiki-fact: the Dutch common name and scientific name (*brandgans* and *branta leucopsis*) apparently derive from Old Norse *Brandgás*, meaning 'burnt goose'.

I'm free on Saturday, so I'm thinking of a plan to perhaps try to head east to catch some migrating cranes. I'm also dying to find a nutcracker, multiple of which have poured down south into Belgium, one seemingly residing for a couple of days now in [Heverleebos](https://waarnemingen.be/locations/28822/). Hard to spot cranes in a dense forest, though.
